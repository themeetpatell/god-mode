# Founder Content

The point of this skill is simple: a post that reads like you ate the breakfast, not like ChatGPT wrote about someone who ate breakfast.

## When to use

- LinkedIn posts (single, carousel, video script)
- Newsletter editions
- X/Twitter threads
- Thought-leadership essays
- Conference/podcast talking points
- Founder updates to investors or community
- Any text published under a founder's name

## When NOT to use

- Sales copy / outreach (use `sales-copywriter`)
- Landing page copy (use `ui-ux-conversion`)
- Internal comms (use `internal-comms`)
- SEO/AEO-optimized blog posts (use `seo-aeo-geo` and chain this one in for the byline)

## The founder voice contract

Every piece must pass these checks:

1. **Lived specificity** — at least 2 concrete proofs per piece (a number, a name, a place, a date, a dollar amount, a story). "I scaled growth" fails. "We added 137 paid accounts in 9 weeks at $14 CAC" passes.
2. **One operator belief** — what is the writer actually claiming? Not three claims, one. If you can't name it in one sentence, the piece isn't ready.
3. **A reason to publish today** — what made this post necessary this week vs last? Trigger > evergreen.
4. **A reader job** — what does the reader do differently after reading?
5. **Sounds spoken** — if you wouldn't say it on a podcast, don't write it.

## The anti-AI-pattern detector (kill on sight)

Every one of these phrases is grounds for rewrite:

| Pattern | Why it's banned | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "In a world where..." | Cinematic opener | A specific moment |
| "In today's fast-paced..." | Slide template | A specific date |
| "Let's dive into" | Filler | Start with the claim |
| "It's not just X, it's Y" | Contrast template (overused since 2024) | Either X or Y, named |
| "Game-changer" / "revolutionary" / "disruptive" | Empty intensifier | The actual mechanism |
| "Unlock the power of" / "harness" | LinkedIn corp-speak | The action |
| "Whether you're A or B" | Audience-stuffing | Pick A |
| "At the end of the day" | Hedge | Cut entirely |
| "Navigate" (when not literal) | AI verb | Actual verb |
| "Leverage" (when not literal) | AI verb | "Use" |
| "Empower" (without subject) | Vague | Who empowers whom to do what |
| "Reach out" | Corporate | "Email", "DM", "call" |
| "Synergy" / "ecosystem" / "transformation" | Buzzwords | The actual thing |
| Em-dash sentence with no specific noun on either side | AI tell since 2023 | Period + new sentence |
| "Three things I learned..." (without listed thing being a real learning) | Listicle template | Lead with the one lesson |
| Numbered insights that are restatements of the same insight | Padding | One insight, fully shown |

If a draft has more than 2 of these, rewrite. More than 4, throw out and start over.

## The lived-specificity counter

Open the draft. Count:
- **Distinct numbers** (not percentages of percentages — actual dollar amounts, dates, counts, durations)
- **Named people** (real people, not "a customer", not "a friend")
- **Named companies** (yours, others)
- **Specific places** (Dubai, Bandra, Y Combinator W22)
- **Dated events** ("the Tuesday after we launched", "March 2024")

Target ≥ 4 across the categories for any piece over 200 words. Under 4 = generic. Rewrite with specifics or kill.

## Format library

### Single LinkedIn post (1,200-2,200 chars)

```
HOOK (line 1, must stop the scroll):
<a claim, a counter-intuitive statement, or a "I shipped X" moment>

BODY (4-8 short paragraphs):
- Open with the moment / trigger
- Show the lived specifics
- State the belief
- Show the proof
- Optional: one redirect ("but actually, the real lesson was...")
- Close with the reader job

CTA (last line):
- A question, or "DM me if X", or "link in comments"
```

Mobile preview cuts at ~210 chars on LinkedIn — the first 3 lines must earn the click.

### Carousel (5-10 slides)

```
Slide 1: HOOK + tagline
Slides 2-N: one idea per slide, ≤ 30 words
Slide N+1: SUMMARY + CTA
```

Each slide should be self-contained. People save carousels for later — if slide 4 needs slide 2's context, the save is wasted.

### Newsletter (800-1,800 words)

```
SUBJECT (matters more than the body): ≤ 50 chars, specific
PREVIEW (first 100 chars): a promise
LEAD (first paragraph): a moment
SECTIONS (2-4): each with a header, each with a payoff
TAKEAWAY (last 50 words): the one thing
P.S.: the link
```

### Thread (Twitter / X, 5-12 tweets)

```
Tweet 1: HOOK + promise of what's in the thread
Tweets 2-N: one beat per tweet, each completing a thought
Tweet N+1: CTA (follow / link / DM)
```

## The trigger library

Don't post unless one of these is true this week:
1. **You shipped something** (the artifact + the lesson)
2. **You learned something the hard way** (the wound + the takeaway)
3. **You changed your mind** (what you used to believe + what changed it)
4. **A pattern is showing up across customers** (n=many, name the pattern)
5. **You ran an experiment** (hypothesis + result + what you'd do differently)
6. **The market moved** (news + your take, with a real opinion)
7. **Someone asked you the same question 3 times** (the question + your answer)

If none of these fire, you don't have a post. Don't manufacture one — that's how generic content happens.

## Worked example

Bad draft (typical AI output):

> In today's fast-paced startup world, founders often struggle with the question of when to hire their first executive. It's not just about timing — it's about finding the right person to leverage your vision. Whether you're scaling a B2B SaaS or a consumer app, the principles remain the same. Let me share three things I learned while making my first executive hire...

Anti-pattern count: 7. Lived specificity count: 0. Rewrite from scratch.

Good rewrite:

> I hired my first Head of Sales 11 weeks too late. We left ~$340K on the table.
>
> Here's what I missed: the second a deal cycle stretches past 5 weeks, you're past the founder-sells window. I was still doing demos in week 9. The pipeline had grown 4× since June, my conversion had halved, and I was the bottleneck.
>
> Fatima joined in October. By week 6 she'd rebuilt our discovery script, killed two products from the deck, and pushed ACV from $14K to $22K on three deals.
>
> Lesson: stop measuring "am I ready to hire" by your calendar. Measure it by the cycle time of your worst-fit deals. When that number doubles, the hire is already late.
>
> If you're a B2B founder and your cycle time has crept past 30 days, DM me — I'll send you the scorecard I wish I'd had.

Anti-patterns: 0. Lived specifics: 7 (11 weeks, $340K, 5 weeks, week 9, 4×, Fatima, October, week 6, $14K, $22K, 3 deals, 30 days). Trigger: a learning the user paid for.

That's founder content.

## Routing

- **Haiku**: generating 3-5 hook variants for a post you've already drafted
- **Sonnet**: default for full drafts + anti-pattern scrubbing
- **Opus**: only when the piece is a high-stakes narrative (fundraising essay, public position on a controversy)

## Verification protocol

The `verifier` (class: content) will:
1. Run the anti-pattern detector on the full text. Reject if count > 2.
2. Run the lived-specificity counter. Reject if < 4 on a piece > 200 words.
3. Confirm exactly one operator belief is stated.
4. Confirm a trigger from the trigger library is present.
5. Confirm a reader job is defined (what does the reader do next).

Conditional pass if 1-2 anti-patterns survive with justification. Fail if >2 or if specificity count is 0.

## Distribution + repurposing

Every long-form piece (newsletter, essay) should ship with:
- A pull-quote graphic for LinkedIn/X
- 3-5 atomic LinkedIn post variants drawn from the piece
- One thread version
- One audio script (3-5 min)
- One sales-enablement clip ("share this with prospects who ask about X")

Mark each as "for use within 7 days" — the trigger ages.
